# Talking Points — Ready-to-Use Language for the Audience

Use these phrases as templates when Claude is interpreting a chart for the audience. Adapt them to fit your specific metrics and story.

---

## Opening Lines (Any Chart)

### Stating the headline

- "[Metric] is down [X]% in [time period]."
- "We're seeing a [X] percentage-point decline in [metric]."
- "[Segment] is the worst performer, down [X]%."
- "Here's what changed: [metric] went from [start] to [end]."

### Adding urgency

- "The trend is *accelerating*—we're moving in the wrong direction faster."
- "It's not just down. It's getting worse month-over-month."
- "We've hit an inflection point. The decline used to be gradual; now it's steep."
- "This didn't happen overnight. It started [timing], and we're feeling it now."

### Giving context

- "Across the board, [metric] is affected."
- "But look closer—it's not evenly distributed."
- "The average hides what's really happening in each segment."
- "This is a [segment]-driven story, not a company-wide story."

---

## Q1 Phrasings: "What is Wrong?"

### The problem statement

- "Your primary challenge right now is [metric]. It's declined [X]% and it's trending worse."
- "The headline: [metric] is down. Here's why it matters to you: [business impact]."
- "If I had to summarize our biggest risk in one metric, it would be [metric]. And it's moving in the wrong direction."

### The timeline

- "[Metric] started declining in [month]. We're now at the lowest point."
- "This has been building for [timespan]. The last [timespan] has been the steepest."
- "It's a steady decline. No sudden cliff, but the trajectory is concerning."

### The stakes

- "If this trend continues at the current rate, [metric] will reach [critical level] in [timespan]."
- "We have [timespan] to act before this becomes unfixable."
- "The cost of inaction is [financial impact] in [timespan]."

---

## Q2 Phrasings: "Where Is It Worst?"

### Identifying the culprit

- "[Segment] is the biggest issue. It's down [X]% while the average is down [Y]%."
- "President's Club is catastrophic—down [X]%. That's what's driving the overall decline."
- "If we fixed [segment], we'd solve [X]% of the problem."

### The contrast

- "But here's what's interesting: [Segment 2] is actually *stable* or *growing*."
- "Enterprise is *up* 5%. So it's not an industry issue. It's specific to [culprit segment]."
- "[Segment 3] is holding steady, which is why the overall number isn't *as bad* as it could be."

### The discovery

- "So the story is: one or two segments are in freefall. Everything else is moderate. That concentration is actually good news—we know where to focus."
- "When we drill by [dimension], the culprit is unmissable. It's not spread out."
- "This tells us it's a [segment]-specific problem, not a product-wide problem."

### The segment story

- "[Culprit segment] is down because: [reason from Q3]."
- "[Counter-trend segment] is up because: [reason from Q3]."
- "The pattern: high-cost segments are getting hit. Low-cost segments are resilient."

---

## Q3 Phrasings: "Why Is It Happening?"

### The causal chain

- "Here's what happened in sequence:"
  - "August: [Supporting metric 1] dropped. That was first."
  - "September: [Supporting metric 2] followed. Engagement broke."
  - "October: [Primary metric] finally fell. By then, it was inevitable."

- "The *leading indicator* is [supporting metric 1]. It always moves first. That's your early warning system."
- "We saw [supporting metric 1] break. We should have acted then. By the time [primary metric] dropped, we'd already lost momentum."

### The root cause

- "When we look at [cost/effort dimension], the pattern is clear: [cause]."
- "[Root cause] is the lever. That's where we need to pull to fix this."
- "It's not a product issue. It's a [pricing/positioning/efficiency] issue."

### The financial or operational angle

- "High-cost clients are abandoning us. Low-cost clients are sticking."
- "Bigger deals are cratering. Smaller deals are solid."
- "Expensive programs are suffering. Lean programs are resilient."
- "Clients who have to staff up to use us are churning. Clients who can do it solo are staying."

---

## Q4 Phrasings: "Who Needs Action?"

### The actionable list

- "Here are the [N] accounts you need to focus on. They represent [X]% of the revenue at risk."
- "This list is your priority. Save these, and we've solved most of the problem."
- "These 15 accounts are where we win or lose. Everyone else on the roster, we can manage."

### Ownership and next steps

- "[Owner] owns [N] of these. [Owner], you're going to want to be in the first calls."
- "Sarah, David, and Michael: you three own 60% of the at-risk revenue. You're on point."
- "Let's start with [Owner]'s list. If we can turn two accounts around there, it sends a signal to the rest."

### Urgency

- "These accounts renew in [timeframe]. We're inside the window to act."
- "If we lose these, we've lost the game. If we save them, we're not out of the woods, but we've bought time."
- "[Account] is the most at risk. It's [metric status] and the owner is [situation]. We should call them this week."

### The ask

- "Your job is to have [N] conversations. The conversations are: 'What changed? How can we help?'"
- "These accounts need proof of value. We need to either change our positioning or change our offer."
- "We have [timeframe] to course-correct with this list. That determines Q4."

## Connecting to Broader Context

### Why this matters (business impact)

- "This isn't just numbers. If we lose [segment], we lose [customer type / revenue tier / strategic asset]."
- "Losing President's Club means losing our premium tier and our brand reputation."
- "If SMB adoption flattens, we've lost our growth engine."
- "This is revenue at risk. The number is $[X]M in [timeframe]."

### Competitive or market context

- "Our competitors are seeing this too, but they're also seeing [different segment] get hit."
- "We're vulnerable because [reason]. Our [segment] clients are looking elsewhere."
- "This is a market shift. We either adapt or we shrink."

### Strategic implications

- "This changes our go-to-market strategy. We need to pivot from [old approach] to [new approach]."
- "The data is telling us: [strategic insight]."
- "We have a choice: do we double down on [segment] or pivot to [different segment]?"

---

## Handling Audience Questions

### "Is this seasonal?"

- "Good question. Let me check the same period last year. [Compare.] No, this is worse than seasonal. It's structural."
- "Normally [metric] dips in [month]. But [year] it was [level]. This year it's [level]. So it's worse than normal."

### "What are other companies seeing?"

- "I don't have that data here, but based on what I know, [competitors/industry] are seeing [pattern]. We're either in line with that or ahead of it."
- "If we're seeing this and they're not, we have a competitive disadvantage. If we're all seeing it, it's market-driven."

### "When can we fix this?"

- "If we act on [root cause] this week, we might see [supporting metric] stabilize in [timeframe]. Then [primary metric] would follow in [timeframe after that]."
- "The lag time is important: [metric 1] is a leading indicator. We see it move first. Give it [timespan] before the primary metric responds."
- "Best case: [timeframe]. Realistic case: [longer timeframe]. Worst case: [worst timeframe]."

### "What if we do nothing?"

- "The trend continues. [Metric] reaches [critical level] in [timeframe]. Revenue impact is [amount]. At that point, damage control becomes harder."
- "We'll have fewer choices. Right now, we can proactively fix it. In [timeframe], it becomes reactive."

---

## Closing Lines (Summary / Call to Action)

- "The good news: the problem is *concentrated* and *identifiable*. We know where it is and why."
- "The urgent action: [specific step]. That's this week. Then [next step]. That's next week."
- "If we get this right, we're not just reversing a decline—we're repositioning for the next phase."
- "Questions before we go into the details on [next segment]?"
